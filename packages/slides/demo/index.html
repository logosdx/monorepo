A tall panel

This panel is taller than the screen. ↓ scrolls it a screen at a time, and only moves on at its bottom edge. A mouse or touch scroll can rest anywhere inside it.

1. Snapping

Columns snap sideways and panels snap vertically, both mandatory. A panel taller than the viewport is still free to scroll, because the snap area is larger than the snapport.

2. Growing

Panels have a minimum height of the viewport and no maximum. Content is never scaled down or clipped to fit a fixed canvas.

3. Reading order

Reading order runs down a column, then on to the next column. Space and PageDown follow it; → skips the rest of a column.

4. Printing

Print turns off snapping and fixed heights, so each panel starts a page and a tall panel flows across as many pages as it needs.

5. Without JavaScript

The stylesheet alone lays out and scrolls the deck. Fragments stay visible until the script marks the deck ready.

6. Keys inside controls

Keys typed into inputs, buttons, and editable regions belong to them, not to the deck.

7. Notes

Speaker notes open in a separate window that the deck writes to directly, so the notes window needs no script of its own.

8. One deck per page

A second <slides> logs an error and is left alone.
